●   LUISA (She Was Born In A Screen)  installation  2  2   Luisa is an animated digital being and AI chatbot. Whether she has human or non-human traits, whether she is alive or not, conscious or not, Luisa is an entity that is born inside a screen, with which it is possible to chat and exchange images. On the one hand, Luisa is   procedurally modeled and animated, thus with completely unexpected appearances and movements including breathing and responsiveness, that mimics the human species. On the other hand, just like the majority of the online world, she is forced to follow limited forms of exchange, imposed by design, by proprietary platforms.

●   Maribor-on-Sea  recovered gif collage  5  5   Maribor-on-Sea is an animated gif collage comprised of thousands of gifs recovered from the geocities archive. It’s an imagined city made in reference to Italo Calvino’s Invisible Cities. A cityscape forever stuck on-loop. Trapped in a moment and forced forever to repeat it. In an installation setting it serves as an “I Spy” exercise, with a long list of descriptors of every single specific gif in the collage accompanying the piece.

●   A GARDEN DOODLE  browser based experiment  7  7   A   Garden Doodle is a generative artwork made in reference to a small child’s doodled paintings that are drawn with chalk or watercolors and end up decorating fridges or the roads of streets in the summertime. The   SketchRNN model doodles human concepts like pool, cat, owl, bear, butterfly and many more, that it has been trained on, trying to arrange them in a composition that could resemble a garden.

●   FINDING LIVABLE CITIES  dissertation project  8  8   A project that was born out of me trying to find the connection between urban planning and design, my homesickness and low mood that I felt living in Manchester. I generated my own dataset of 70 thousand maps of urban centres around the world and trained a few versions of Variational Autoencoders, to try to understand and unravel the incredibly complex forms and arrangements of the built environments. To visualize the latent space embeddings of the models I used the UMAP dimensionality reduction algorithm, as seen above deployed to a website. I wanted the model to learn the latent characteristics of cities and help me explain why I disliked Manchester. In this I failed, but have since grown to love the city either way.

●   LIFE CAPTIONER  browser based installation  17  17   A real time webcam captioning system powered by a whimsical jailbroken version of ChatGPT4. The prompt instructs ChatGPT to impersonate a   terminally online redditor while describing what can be seen on the camera feed. The deeper postulation of this project was the   complete semantic encoding of one's life, that systems like chatgpt are now rendering possible. One could walk around with a GoPro camera and feed the video to a captioning system like the one I put together and so have their daily life transcribed into searchable text, every moment of their life recorded.

●   SUPERINTERNET.WORLD  browser based collaborative 3D world for artists  18  18   A 3D world in the browser arranged into a grid of many rooms with blank canvases for walls, that artists can fill with their own artworks with a microsoft-paint-like drawing mechanic. Every room is its own painting, which unravels into its   own  beautiful atlas. This atlas sits on the   Polygon blockchain network, where artists can subsequently be adequately compensated for their work. The project is open to non-artist browser-based audiences as well.

●   WEIRDATTACHMENTS.ONLINE  self-publishing book website  19  19   A publishing project commissioned by a Ljubljana based artist exhibited at MoTA. The website includes a fully-custom rich-text editor with all the traditional book-publishing affordances such as epigraphs, poetry blocks, footnotes, chapter titles and subtitles, custom backgrounds

per-chapter/page, custom page frames, as well as a real-time karaoke-style audio-text highlighting function. The desktop website immitates the form of a pdf-reader (attachment reader), while the mobile version takes the form of an “elevated kindle” reader. The client can update the website as much as she wants and every update will generate a new version of the book, a new attachment.
